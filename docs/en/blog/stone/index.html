Stone: from a patchwork of systems to one event platform for 1,500 developers

How Stone’s platform team standardized card, PIX and virtual-payment streaming on Confluent Cloud.

{{ cover }}

Context

Stone is one of Brazil’s largest payment companies, serving merchants with card terminals, digital accounts, PIX and software solutions. Inside engineering, a horizontal platform team is responsible for providing a robust, scalable and reliable messaging solution to more than 1,500 developers who use it to build event-driven applications.

That team is the backbone of the real-time data infrastructure and directly supports the company’s revenue streams: physical card transactions, virtual payments and PIX transfers all run through it. In the words of SRE manager Allan Oliveira, the team is “the engine that allows Stone to move quickly in a competitive fintech landscape, while maintaining the reliability that financial services demand”.

The problem

Stone’s event-streaming journey began years ago and grew organically, with several different platforms living side by side. By 2023 the landscape was a patchwork: different technologies per area, hand-built integrations, unstandardized data formats and a maintenance effort that grew with every new system connected.

Despite the platform team’s vital role, that fragmentation slowed the response to business needs. Launching a new product or integrating a partner meant dealing with several tools, each with its own operations, limits and failure modes. It became clear the company needed a standardized, scalable, cloud-agnostic solution for its real-time data flows.

The solution

Stone began its partnership with Confluent and adopted Confluent Cloud as its single streaming platform. Two components solved most of the pain: managed connectors, which replaced hand-made integrations and move data to and from databases and systems with no maintenance code, and Schema Registry, which now guarantees data integrity between producers and consumers, preventing a format change in one team from breaking the others.

On the operations side, auto-scaling now absorbs volume swings, and managing clusters through the Confluent interface removed tasks from the SRE team that used to require scripts and maintenance windows. Oliveira sums it up: what sold him on the managed approach was stability; auto-scaling “works really well”, and handling cluster management through the interface “is a game-changer” for the team.

Results

Integrations now happen faster, data moves freely between systems and the maintenance load dropped. With one platform, the 1,500 developers build on the same bus with shared data contracts instead of learning several tools.

Confluent cites Stone, alongside iFood, as one of the cases that motivated the company’s team expansion in Brazil, describing the modernization “from a patchwork of systems to a unified streaming platform” that lets it keep delivering best-in-class technology to the financial market.

What this means for your team

In payments, consolidating platforms is as much a governance project as a technology one. What lets 1,500 people build on the same bus without breaking each other is not Kafka itself but the data contracts enforced in Schema Registry and the managed connectors that take integration off the critical path.

We see the same patchwork in Brazilian banks and fintechs: MQ on one side, open-source Kafka on another, cloud queues on a third. Our architecture work starts with an inventory of those platforms and a wave plan to converge without stopping operations.
