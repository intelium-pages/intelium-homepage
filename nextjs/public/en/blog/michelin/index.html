Michelin: 35% savings by moving Kafka to Confluent Cloud

How the tire maker scaled Kafka, adopted cloud-native streaming and started running inventory in real time.

{{ cover }}

Context

Michelin is one of the largest tire makers in the world and is repositioning itself as a sustainable mobility solutions company, with data-driven services such as predicting when a tire needs replacing and the best routes to save fuel.

The problem

The company already used Kafka, but running the platform itself at global scale consumed the team’s time and budget. Every new application depended on capacity, upgrades and support that competed with product work.

The solution

According to Confluent, Michelin moved its Kafka deployments to Confluent Cloud and now treats streaming as an essential part of its data infrastructure, letting data flow in real time between systems. On that foundation the company runs real-time inventory management and an omnichannel experience, and is moving toward an event-driven information system.

Results

Confluent documents 35% savings with Kafka on Confluent Cloud compared with the previous setup, along with real-time inventory and an omnichannel experience. The group’s Chief Digital & Information Officer describes Confluent Cloud as an essential piece of the company’s data infrastructure.

What this means for your team

Michelin’s math is the one we see most in our assessments: the cost of self-managed Kafka is not just machines, it is the engineering hours spent on upgrades, capacity and incidents. When that cost is put next to the managed service, the migration usually pays for itself.

The thing to get right is the path: migrate with Cluster Linking, with no downtime window and consumers switching clusters in stages.
