Capital One: real-time banking experiences for 100 million customers

How a tech-first bank uses data streaming for personalization, fraud detection and cybersecurity.

{{ cover }}

Context

Capital One is one of the largest banks in the United States and one of the first big banks to call itself a technology company that runs a bank. It serves around 100 million customers across cards, accounts, credit and digital services, and completed a full migration to the public cloud, closing its data centers.

That tech-first strategy puts real-time data at the center: every transaction, login, app interaction and network signal is an event that can feed a product, risk or security decision at the same instant.

The problem

Fraud and cyberattacks happen in seconds. A cloned card is used several times within minutes; an account-takeover attempt produces dozens of signals in sequence. Models and rules fed by yesterday’s data, in nightly batches, detect the problem after the loss has already happened.

The same applies to customer experience: a relevant offer or alert only has value if it arrives in the right context, at the moment the customer is acting, not the next day.

The solution

According to Confluent, Capital One uses its data streaming platform to power real-time innovation: personalized and secure experiences for millions of customers, including fraud detection and cybersecurity on events as they happen. Transaction and security-signal streams are processed as they arrive, feeding models that decide before the operation completes.

The same event base serves both ends: protecting the customer and serving them better. It is the tech-first strategy made concrete in financial services, with streaming as the common layer between channels, risk and product.

Results

Real-time banking experiences for about 100 million customers, with fraud and cybersecurity handled in the event flow rather than in later batches. Confluent presents the case as an example of how a technology-centered strategy uses data streaming to drive innovation at a large bank.

Architecture details and internal figures are not public; what Confluent documents is the scope and the business outcome. We treat the case here as a reference for direction, not as a blueprint.

What this means for your team

With PIX, Brazil’s fraud decision window is seconds: the transfer settles within ten seconds and is irrevocable. No nightly batch solves that. The pattern is the same as Capital One’s: transaction events, enrichment with history and context in Flink, a model score and a decision before settlement.

It is the use case we implement most often in banks. What is usually missing is not the model but the right data at the right moment: features computed in the stream, with up-to-date customer context, instead of a feature store that reflects yesterday.
